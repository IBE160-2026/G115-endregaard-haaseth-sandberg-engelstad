# Domain research: reference projects and event sources

**Decision this research serves:** whether the adviser can name real comparable projects (PRD §11 Q3), which reference values and phase shares it may use (FR-9, FR-15), and what sources back each event in the event pool (FR-19). This builds on the earlier research report. That report's parameter table (P11 total time, P12 frame time per floor) is not repeated here; this report tests it against new projects.

All sources were accessed on 2026-10-31. NOK amounts exclude VAT unless stated.

## Executive summary

- **The answer to PRD §11 Q3 is yes, with limits.** Six completed Norwegian office projects in the size range have enough public data to cite, plus one Oslo project under construction. Only two of them are in Oslo (Valle Wood and HasleTre, both CLT). No completed Oslo **cast-in-place concrete** office of 1,000–10,000 m² with a published construction time was found. The adviser should therefore use named projects for CLT and steel/prefab, and describe concrete generically.
- **The best comparables for a 2,300 m² building:**
  - Statens hus Nordfjordeid: 1,880 m² BRA, steel + hollow-core, **about 11 months** [9].
  - HasleTre, Oslo: 2,655 m², CLT, **~106 MNOK (2023)**; construction time not published [4].
  - Youngs gate 2–4, Oslo: ~2,700 m², **planned Q1 2025 to Q4 2026** (about 21 months), 100 MNOK [11]. This is a central-Oslo infill site, so it is a useful upper anchor, but it is not yet completed.
- **Phase shares:** no Norwegian or Nordic published table of duration shares by phase was found, and that absence is itself a finding. The only primary phase dates come from Elvebredden (11,000 m², steel + hollow-core). They give roughly **groundworks 24% · frame 26% · envelope to weathertight 24% · interior/technical 19% · commissioning/handover 10%** of a 21-month build [10]. The shares are [ESTIMATE]s anchored on that one project.
- **CLT frame speed needs a correction.** The earlier P12 value (0.6 wk per floor) came from a residential CLT block. Valle Wood, an office with beams and columns, planned **2.5 weeks per ~960 m² floor and took longer**, because the crane had to hold beams until they were bolted [1]. Suggest a CLT range of 0.6–1.5 wk per 600 m² floor.
- **The optimism-bias event is well sourced.** Across buildings, the average schedule overrun is **32%** and the average cost overrun **62%**, and 7 of 10 projects overrun [15]. This supports both the "main part takes longer" event and the adviser's warning threshold.
- **Well sourced events:** optimism (1), archaeology in the medieval town (4), material prices 2021–22 (6), contractor bankruptcies (7), change requests (8), CLT moisture (10) and the Høyblokka weathered rock. **Partly sourced:** crane wind (2), foreign strike (3) and contaminated ground (5): the event itself is documented, but the delay figures are estimates. **Weak:** frost/winter concreting (9), with no Norwegian time or cost figure found.
- **Regulatory support for the adviser's method:** Oslobygg's 2025 form for documenting sufficient construction time asks the client to list comparable projects used to estimate construction time [13]. Reference-class reasoning is official practice in Oslo, not only a game device.

### A2. What the projects say about the earlier parameters

- **P11 total time (12 months for ~2,300 m²) holds for a simple site.** Statens hus took 11 months for 1,880 m² BRA [9], and Norva24 and Stamas took 9–11 months. For a **central Oslo infill site**, Youngs gate plans about 21 months for 2,700 m² [11]. The game's central plot (A) should therefore get a longer base duration than the outer plot (C). The size of the difference is an [ESTIMATE]: +4 to +8 months.
- **P12 frame time per floor:**
  - **Steel + hollow-core:** Elvebredden took about 3 weeks per ~1,400 m² floor [10]. At a ~460–600 m² floor plate that is about 1–1.3 weeks, consistent with P12 (1.0 wk).
  - **CLT:** P12 (0.6 wk) is too optimistic for an office. Valle Wood planned 2.5 weeks per ~960 m² floor, about 1.5 weeks per 600 m², and still overran [1]. Suggested CLT range: 0.6–1.5 wk; use **1.0 wk** as the base [ESTIMATE].
- **Cost per m² (cross-check of P6 = 35,000):** the projects span 24,000–40,000 NOK/m² (2018–2025 prices, mixed scope):
  - Lumber 5: 24,000 [8]
  - Elvebredden: 28,600 [10]
  - Valle Wood: ~29,900 [1]
  - Youngs gate: 37,000 [11]
  - HasleTre: ~40,000 [4]

  The Oslo projects (HasleTre, Youngs gate) are at the top of the range, which supports P6 = 35,000 for Oslo.

## A3. Phase shares of construction time

**Finding:** no Norwegian or Nordic published table of how construction time splits across phases (design/permit, groundworks, frame, envelope, technical, interior, handover) was found in open sources. Norwegian and English searches returned only vendor blogs and cost shares, which are not time shares.

**Primary anchors found:**

| Source | Milestones | Derived shares (lead calculation) |
|---|---|---|
| Elvebredden [10] | Groundworks start Aug 2023 · frame Jan–Jun 2024 · weathertight Nov 2024 · mechanically complete Mar 2025 · handover May 2025 | Groundworks ~5 mo (24%) · frame ~5.5 mo (26%) · envelope to weathertight ~5 mo (24%) · interior/technical to mech. complete ~4 mo (19%) · commissioning/handover ~2 mo (10%) of ~21 months |
| Statens hus [9] | Groundworks Mar 2024 · first pour early Apr 2024 · handover 28 Feb 2025 | Total ~11 months; no intermediate milestones |
| Oslo kommune [14] | Statutory processing time for a framework permit (rammetillatelse): 12 weeks (pbl § 21-7) | Permit phase ≥ 12 weeks |
| Oslobygg form [13] | Must include design time, contractor mobilisation and ordering time, a test/commissioning period, and an allowance for ground uncertainty | Confirms the phase list; gives no numbers |

The phases overlap in reality (technical rough-in starts before weathertight). The game's sequential Gantt is a simplification.

## D. How the adviser may phrase citations

1. **Name, figure, source and year, in one sentence.** For example: "Statens hus Nordfjordeid (1,880 m², steel and hollow-core) was built in about 11 months (Byggeindustrien, 2025)." Never cite a figure that is "unknown" in table A1. For HasleTre, cite cost and size, never duration.
2. **Say what kind of figure it is.** "Planned" for R4, R7 and R8 ("Youngs gate in Oslo is planned at about 21 months"). "Reported" for actuals. "Estimate" for V3–V7, V9–V10 and every [ESTIMATE] impact.
3. **Scale explicitly when sizes differ.** "Elvebredden's frame took about 3 weeks per floor, but its floors are more than twice the size of yours." Do not present a per-floor figure as if it transfers directly.
4. **For cast-in-place concrete, use a generic description**, because no named Oslo comparable exists: "Cast-in-place concrete frames typically take about twice as long as timber (TU, 2015)."
5. **Events cite the real case once, then the game effect.** "Late changes cost on average about 14% extra in ten Norwegian projects (NTNU, 2023). This change adds X." Keep the real case and the game number visibly separate.
6. **Fictional client, real comparables.** The client in the scenario stays fictional. The adviser only cites public facts about the real projects, and never implies that those firms are part of the scenario.

# PRD Quality Review — Fra tomt til tak

- **PRD:** `01. Admin/planning-artifacts/prds/prd-fra-tomt-til-tak-2026-10-31/prd.md`
- **Rubric:** `.claude/skills/bmad-prd/assets/prd-validation-checklist.md`
- **Reviewed:** 2026-10-31 (rubric walker, read-only)
- **Context applied:** graded IBE160 project; the examiner checks that code traces to requirements. Per `.memlog.md`, Open Questions 1 (main parts) and 4 (reference projects) wait on research in progress, and the group keeps the full must-have scope at ~80 hours and will reassess later. These are treated as known open items, not defects. Comments below only cover how they are recorded.

## Overall verdict

The PRD has a clear, specific thesis: optimistic estimates, concept choices and event responses add up to an overrun the student owns, and the AI is a coach to weigh, not obey. It is lean, non-theatrical, well scoped for a student project, and has honest counter-metrics. What is at risk is the core mechanic. The PRD does not say how the schedule estimate turns into actual durations in stage 2, or how the budget bridge assigns causes. Five of the nine debrief FRs (FR-23, FR-25 to FR-28) have no testable consequences, and those FRs carry the learning goals SM-2 measures. The concept step (§4.2) also describes a trade-off that the specified cost rules cannot produce. Fix these before epics and stories, because story creation will stall exactly there.

## Decision-readiness — adequate

Real decisions are stated as decisions, and the important trade-offs are named. §10 records the departure from the brief: crew choices move from stage 1 to stage 2, and it says where the "resources have limits" goal now lives. §8.2 is honest that FR-17 (free-text LLM) is should-have because "prepared answers cover the must-have". FR-13 makes a clear call: a plan that misses targets *can* be approved, and the warning is logged. The 20 % warning threshold in FR-15 is a concrete decision. §11 contains questions that are actually open, with no rhetorical ones.

The main gap is that the most important project-level trade-off is invisible in the PRD. The memlog records capacity cut from ~120 to ~80 hours and a deliberate decision to keep the full must-have scope (31 FRs including 3D, Gantt, debrief charts and an adviser) and reassess later. A reader of the PRD alone, including the examiner, would not know about this decision or the risk it carries. Only two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts exist (FR-7 and NFR-2), and neither sits at this tension.

### Findings
- **medium** Capacity and scope decision not recorded in the PRD (§8 MVP Scope) — The memlog decision "keep the full must-have scope despite ~80 hours; reassess once pace is known" exists only in `.memlog.md`. §8 reads as if scope and capacity are not in tension. This is the decision the group has knowingly deferred, so it should be visible where a reader looks for scope. *Fix:* add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` in §8.1 that states the ~80-hour budget, the choice to keep full scope, the reassessment trigger (for example, after the first epic), and which FRs would go first if cut (for example FR-28, FR-23 overlay, the 3D progress in FR-18).
- **low** The difficulty setting's interaction with the "correct" adviser is not surfaced (§4.1 FR-1, §4.6) — FR-1 keeps v0.5 difficulty, which "sets how often the AI's stage 2 recommendation is the best one". §4.6 says the adviser's suggestions "are correct and testable". So the PRD has two AIs with different reliability, which is a meaningful design choice for the learning goal "AI advice is worth weighing". It is never named as a deliberate decision. *Fix:* state it explicitly in §4.6 or §10: the stage 1 adviser is always rule-correct, and stage 2 recommendations are deliberately fallible by difficulty, and explain why.

## Substance over theater — strong

There is little furniture here. One persona (Sara) drives concrete decisions: no construction background, motivated by news of overruns, and her optimistic frame estimate is the UJ's climax. The Vision could not be swapped into another PRD: "office building for 100 employees in Oslo", "why do building projects so often run late". The Non-Goals in §7 do real work, for example "Building one's own WBS from scratch" and "Detailed crew and staffing planning". The counter-metrics in §9 are thoughtful and specific to the product, for example SM-C1, which declines to optimize for the rate of following the AI.

NFRs are mostly specific (NFR-1, NFR-2, NFR-3, NFR-8). NFR-4 and NFR-5 slip toward adjectives; see Done-ness.

## Strategic coherence — adequate

The thesis is explicit in §1 and runs through the features: estimate (§4.3), get tested (§4.7), get explained (§4.8), with SM-2 checking learning per goal and counter-metrics balancing each primary metric. The MVP kind is clearly a problem-solving/experience MVP, and the scope logic fits.

Two places weaken the arc. First, the mechanic that links the thesis's central claim ("their own optimistic estimates … add up to a result they own") to stage 2 is underspecified. §4.3 says "the schedule estimate is what stage 2 tests", but no FR says what the *actual* duration of a main part is (the reference value? reference plus noise? only for the one overrun event?). FR-19 allows only one estimate overrun per playthrough, and only on a part estimated below reference. Second, the §4.2 concept trade-off as written cannot be produced by the cost rules FR-3 specifies, so the concept step risks having one dominant answer.

### Findings
- **high** No rule links the schedule estimate to actual durations (§4.3, §4.7 FR-18/FR-19, §4.8 FR-24) — The thesis depends on optimistic estimates causing overruns. But FR-18 only says the project "advances week by week from the plan". FR-19 makes the overrun a single seeded event that "hits a main part where the student's schedule estimate is below the reference value". It is undefined what happens to other underestimated parts, how large the overrun is (gap to reference? fixed?), and what happens if the student estimates every part at or above reference (then no part qualifies for the mandatory overrun). The budget bridge's "schedule estimate" cause cannot be computed without this rule. *Fix:* add an FR in §4.7 or §4.9 that defines actual duration per main part (for example "actual = reference value; parts estimated below reference overrun by the gap, and the largest gap triggers the overrun event"), and define the case where every estimate is at or above reference (for example, the event hits the part with the smallest margin, or a different event replaces it).
- **high** The §4.2 trade-off contradicts FR-3's cost rule; the concept step may have a dominant choice (§4.2 description, FR-3, FR-4) — The description says "fewer floors need a larger footprint and a more expensive plot". But FR-3 prices land per m² of the *building's* BRA (research P4: land = price × BRA). So land cost depends only on which plot is chosen, not on floors or footprint. With the research values (P4a–c, P5a–c), all three plots fit 2,300 m², and the outer plot is cheapest by ~55 MNOK. Nothing in the PRD gives the expensive plots a compensating benefit, so choosing plot C every time looks dominant. Also, every feasible concept needs at least 3 floors (largest footprint 800 m²), so FR-4's "two or more floors add the cost of lifts" never discriminates. This ties into Open Question 3, but the contradiction lies in the requirements, not only in the values. *Fix:* either change the land rule (for example, price per m² of plot area, so a larger footprint costs more), or name the counterweight for cheaper plots in an FR (longer transport or logistics time, more ground risks per FR-10, a lower client-value rating), and correct the §4.2 description to match.
- **medium** SM-2 has no baseline (§9 SM-2) — "Testers answer at least 3 of 4 short questions correctly" after a playthrough does not show that the *simulator* taught anything, since testers might answer correctly beforehand. *Fix:* add a pre-playthrough question set (or a non-playing comparison tester) and state the target as an improvement, or justify why post-only is enough for the course.

## Done-ness clarity — thin

Stage 1 FRs are mostly good. FR-4, FR-6, FR-13, FR-15 and FR-22 have crisp, testable consequences. FR-15 is exemplary: the 20 % threshold, the warning states the reference value, it is deterministic and it is logged. FR-24's "parts add up exactly" is a good invariant.

The debrief is where this dimension breaks down, and the debrief carries the learning goals and SM-2. FR-23, FR-25, FR-26, FR-27 and FR-28 have **no** "Consequences (testable)" block. Several also use terms with no definition. For FR-27, nothing says how a goal is "touched" or "missed". For FR-28, nothing says what makes a stage 1 adviser suggestion "right". Elsewhere, several consequences are thin or untestable as written.

### Findings
- **high** Debrief FRs lack testable consequences, and "missed goal" / "right" are undefined (§4.8 FR-23, FR-25, FR-26, FR-27, FR-28) — An engineer cannot tell when FR-27 is done: which choices map to which learning goal, and what threshold counts as "missed"? FR-28 "how often those were right": right by what measure, compared with the AI path's outcome, or with the eventual result? For stage 1 suggestions, which the PRD calls always correct, what does "right" mean at all? FR-23, FR-25 and FR-26 are display FRs with no stated contents or deviation rule. *Fix:* add consequences to each. FR-23: deviation marked when actual end differs from planned end by ≥1 week. FR-25: each known risk listed with response and occurred yes/no. FR-26: completion week and cost shown for both paths. FR-27: a table mapping decision types to the four goals, with a rule per goal for "missed" (for example "time costs money: missed if the student chose to accept delay when overtime was cheaper over the remaining project"). FR-28: define "right" as "the recommended option's outcome was at least as good as the alternative chosen, measured on cost + delay at the end of the playthrough".
- **high** Budget-bridge attribution method undefined (§4.8 FR-24) — The bridge splits the difference into "the schedule estimate, events, the student's decisions, and the reserve used", but gives no rule for allocating a cost to one cause rather than another. Event costs and decision costs are entangled (overtime is a decision made *because of* an event). Also "reserve used" is a buffer, not a cause of overrun. The additivity test can be satisfied by any allocation, including a meaningless one. This chart is "the main device" (memlog) and is validated by SM-2. *Fix:* define each bar. For example: estimate = cost effect of (actual − estimated) durations at plan rates; events = direct event costs under the cheapest option; decisions = the difference between chosen option and cheapest option; reserve = shown as an offset. State the order of attribution.
- **medium** Risk response effects and the link between known risks and events are undefined (§4.4 FR-10/FR-11, §4.7 FR-19/FR-21) — FR-11 says each response has "its cost and effect", but only "avoid" has a testable consequence (FR-21). Nothing says what "reduce" or "transfer" do numerically. It is also unclear whether stage 2's "external risks" (FR-19) are drawn *only* from the 3–4 known risks or from a wider catalogue. The Vision promises "unforeseen events", and UJ-1's strike does not look like a listed risk. *Fix:* in FR-11, state the effect of each response type (for example reduce = probability ×0.5 at cost X; transfer = cost borne by a third party, delay remains). In FR-19, say whether external risks may include unlisted events, and if so, how many.
- **medium** Reserve semantics contradict each other (§4.4 FR-12) — "The reserve is included in the plan's completion week and cost" and "delays and extra costs draw on the reserve before they exceed the targets" are inconsistent. If the reserve is already inside the plan's completion week, then a plan plus reserve that meets the target has already used the target's slack. It is unclear whether the bridge compares results with the plan or with the targets. *Fix:* define plan completion = sum of estimates + reserve, and state that overruns first consume the reserve, then breach the targets. Or keep the reserve separate from the plan figure, and pick one.
- **medium** NFR-4 and NFR-5 use adjectives (§5) — "smooth frame rate on an ordinary student laptop" and "readable contrast" are not bounds. The 30 fps assumption helps, but the reference hardware is undefined. *Fix:* NFR-4: ≥30 fps at 1080p on integrated graphics (name a class, for example Intel Iris Xe), plus the condition for auto quality reduction. NFR-5: WCAG 2.1 AA contrast (4.5:1).
- **low** FR-17 consequences are not testable for an LLM, and "nearest prepared answer" is undefined (§4.6 FR-17) — "The answer never states a figure that differs from the building-block model" cannot be proven for model output. Matching to the "nearest" answer has no stated method. It is should-have only, so low. *Fix:* restate it as a guard, for example "numbers in the model's answer are checked against the approved facts and the answer is replaced with a prepared answer on mismatch", and name the matching method (keyword or embedding).
- **low** FR-2 and FR-7 are thin (§4.1 FR-2, §4.3 FR-7) — FR-2 has no length bound for "short meeting". FR-7's only consequence is determinism, which is acceptable while Open Question 1 is pending. *Fix:* FR-2: meeting ≤1 minute or ≤N dialogue steps. FR-7: revisit when OQ1 closes.

## Scope honesty — adequate

Omissions are explicit. §2.2 Non-Users, §7 Non-Goals, §8.2 with tier labels (should/could/later extension), and FR-17's "Out of Scope" block all do real work. Three assumptions are tagged inline and indexed. Six Open Questions remain, which is a reasonable density for a draft PRD in a course project, especially since two of them (OQ1, OQ4) are knowingly waiting on research.

On how the known open items are recorded: OQ1 is well recorded. FR-7 carries a `[NOTE FOR PM]` that points to §11, and §11 says what will decide it. OQ4 is recorded in §11 but **not** at the FR that depends on it. FR-15's must-have consequence "names at least one real comparable project … Every project cited is listed with its source in the research" depends on that research, and nothing says what happens if the research finds no suitable project for some main part. The capacity decision is not recorded at all (see Decision-readiness).

### Findings
- **medium** FR-15 depends on pending research with no callout or fallback (§4.6 FR-15, §11 OQ4) — A must-have testable consequence depends on `research-referanseprosjekter-2026-10-31`, but the FR does not say so, and there is no fallback when no sourced project exists for a main part. *Fix:* add `[NOTE FOR PM: depends on OQ4]` at FR-15, plus a fallback consequence, for example "if no sourced project exists for a main part, the warning cites the reference value and its source without naming a project".
- **low** Interpretation flag from the memlog not carried over (§8.2, FR-17) — The memlog marks FR-17's optional tier as "[interpretation: optional tier, to confirm]". The PRD states it as settled. *Fix:* confirm with the group and drop the flag, or add an `[ASSUMPTION]` at §8.2 and index it.

## Downstream usability — adequate

This PRD is chain-top (UX, architecture, epics, stories, and examiner traceability), so this dimension matters. The positives: a thorough Glossary in §3, contiguous FR-1..FR-31, NFR-1..8 and SM-1..4 IDs, "Realizes UJ-1" on every user-facing FR, and §0 stating the traceability purpose. The weak spots are a broken cross-reference, a few domain terms used without definition, and blurred naming of the AI entities.

### Findings
- **medium** "Adviser" and "the AI" are blurred (§3, §4.6, §4.7 FR-22, §4.8 FR-28) — The Glossary defines the Adviser as "the AI in its own window" in the planning view. Stage 2 then refers to "the AI's recommendation" (FR-22), FR-28 says "the adviser and the AI's recommendations", and FR-1 says "the AI's stage 2 recommendation". "AI recommendation" is not a glossary term, and it is unclear whether stage 2 recommendations come from the adviser window or from a separate component. That matters for architecture and for FR-28's counting. *Fix:* add the glossary term "AI recommendation (stage 2)", state whether the adviser window is present in stage 2, and use the two terms consistently.
- **low** Domain terms used without definition (§3 vs §4.2, §4.3, UJ-1) — BTA, BRA, %BYA, footprint, gross area, WBS, Gantt chart, critical path and HMS are used but not defined. HMS is a Norwegian acronym in an English document. *Fix:* add short glossary entries, for example "HMS: health, safety and environment (Norwegian HSE)", BTA/BRA with P1/P2, and footprint.
- **low** Vague parameter references (§4.2 FR-3, FR-5) — "P-parameters in the research" and "according to the parameter table" do not say which parameters. *Fix:* cite IDs: FR-3 → P2, P4a–c, P5a–c; FR-4 → P3, P9; FR-5 → P7, P12.

## Shape fit — strong

This is a single-protagonist educational simulator with meaningful UX, and it uses one well-developed UJ with a named protagonist, entry state, climax and edge case. That is the right weight. A second UJ for the examiner would be overhead, and the examiner's JTBD in §2.1 plus NFR-1 and SM-4 covers it. The capability-spec style FRs in §4.9 (FR-30, FR-31) correctly omit "Realizes UJ-1". Rigor is appropriate for a graded chain-top PRD. §10 handles the brownfield aspect (v0.5 reuse) explicitly and separates reused from new parts. Its references to v0.5 ("as in v0.5", "v0.5 rules for work modes, crowding and cost factors") are only as accurate as v0.5's documentation, so downstream readers need a pointer to it.

### Findings
- **low** v0.5 references have no pointer (§4.1 FR-1, §4.7, §10) — "As in v0.5" is load-bearing for difficulty and for the stage 2 work-mode rules, but no file is named. The memlog names `G115_FinalPrompt.md`. *Fix:* cite the v0.5 source file and section in §10, and refer to it from FR-1 and §4.7.
